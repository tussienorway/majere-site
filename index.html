Does an internal "council" of personas make language agents more consistent, better calibrated and easier to control?

Majere: Multi-Persona Deliberation ("Archetype Council") as an Architecture for Affect-Modulated, Self-Monitoring Language Agents.

Abstract

In Majere, several archetype personas deliberate before an agent acts. They share a numerical affect state (valence and arousal), and a single impulsivity parameter decides how much deliberation happens. We test four falsifiable claims: that the affect state makes behaviour more consistent, that impulsivity gives a controllable speed–quality trade-off, that the council improves calibration and reduces self-contradiction, and that functional indicators from consciousness science depend on specific components. Every comparison is against baselines with the same token budget, such as self-consistency and multi-agent debate. If the council does not beat them, the hypothesis is rejected and we publish that result. We make no claim that the system has feelings or consciousness.

Why fund this

  • Falsifiable by design. There is a pre-stated rejection criterion, and the baselines get the same budget.
  • Cheap to test, expensive to scale. The pilot runs on a 7–8B open model. Compute is needed to check whether the effects hold at 30–70B.
  • Open by default. Protocol, evaluation code, configs, raw outputs and negative results are all published under open licences.
  • Safety-relevant. The work covers calibration, abstention and controllable deliberation, and it treats consciousness claims with care instead of hype.
