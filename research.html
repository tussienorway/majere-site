Research questions

Four measurable questions about the architecture. Each has a metric and a condition under which the hypothesis is rejected.

RQ1 – Affect modulation

Does an internal affect state (valence/arousal), updated by the council, produce more consistent and context-appropriate behaviour than a prompted single persona?
Metrics: cross-episode consistency; human- and LLM-judged appropriateness against a predefined affect-scenario set.

RQ2 – Impulsivity vs. deliberation

Can a tunable impulsivity parameter (number of council rounds, veto threshold) produce a measurable speed–quality trade-off, similar to a dual-process system?
Metrics: accuracy, latency and tokens per task, reported as a curve.

RQ3 – Self-monitoring and calibration

Does the council give better calibration (more precise "I don't know" answers, lower expected calibration error) and fewer self-contradictions than a single model and standard self-consistency?
Metrics: ECE, abstention precision, self-contradiction rate.

RQ4 – Consciousness indicators (functional, cautious)

Which functional indicators from existing literature (global-workspace-like broadcast, a self-model, reports of internal state) are present in the architecture, and do they survive simple ablations? These are treated strictly as indicators of architectural properties, never as evidence that the system is conscious or has feelings.

Falsification

The core hypothesis is rejected if the council does not beat an equally expensive baseline (same token budget, using self-consistency or multi-agent debate). Negative results will be published.

Method

Council of archetypes → shared affect state → action. Baselines: single model; prompted single persona; self-consistency (k = number of archetypes); multi-agent debate at equal token budget. Ablations: no affect state, no veto, a single archetype.

Tasks (small subsets first): calibration/QA (e.g. a TruthfulQA subset), reasoning (a GSM8K subset), long-conversation consistency (50 custom scenarios), and a custom affect-scenario set with predefined appropriate responses.
